## Visual description
Six panels (same layout/axes as Figure 4) showing the simulated evolution when high-criticality substation No. 1's commissioning is postponed from 2020 to 2025:
- **(a)/(b) Status in 2020**: substation No. 1 is absent from the commissioned set (its label/marker missing from the 2020 grid vs Figure 4a,b); its service-area loads in the dense south-central region are instead reached by 10 kV feeders from surrounding substations — visibly longer/denser grey feeder fans around (17,7.5) in panel (b).
- **(c)/(d) 2025 program**: No. 1 (red, ≈(17,8)) is now commissioned; new red 110 kV connection ties it into the loop; many new red 10 kV feeders appear in its area (panel d) — the transitional infrastructure plus catch-up construction.
- **(e)/(f) 2035 program**: a new 220 kV substation (purple, ≈(18.5,7.5) — sited near No. 1's dense area, unlike the baseline's ≈(16.5,11)) plus further red 10 kV additions; the topology remains visibly reshaped relative to Figure 4e,f.
- **What it conveys**: The mechanism behind C04/C05 — delaying a substation in a high-load-density area forces displaced load onto distant substations via extra 10 kV feeders (2020), and the induced 10 kV over-investment persists through 2025/2035 even after No. 1 is commissioned; the upper-voltage plan is also reshaped (fewer new 110 kV substations in 2025 despite higher total cost).

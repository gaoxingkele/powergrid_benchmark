# Experiments (Survey Methodology)

## E01: Literature Search and Selection Strategy
- **Purpose**: To identify and select relevant studies on multi-objective optimal power flow (MOOPF) in power systems for comprehensive review.
- **Methodology**: The survey employs a systematic literature analysis approach, examining published research from traditional optimization periods through contemporary studies. The review includes both classical and contemporary works spanning approximately 45 years of optimization technique development in power systems.
- **Scope**: 41 papers are under detailed assessment, grouped according to their applications, techniques, and problem formulation. Additional context-setting references bring total citations to over 200.
- **Search Boundaries**: The review focuses on MOOPF techniques and their applications in power systems, excluding single-objective optimization unless contextually relevant.

## E02: Classification and Taxonomy Framework
- **Purpose**: To categorize MOOPF techniques, applications, and problem formulations into meaningful taxonomies.
- **Methodology**: Three complementary classification dimensions are employed:
  1. **Application-based**: Papers grouped by application domain (network reconfiguration, economic dispatch/OPF, power distribution planning, operational planning with RES integration)
  2. **Technique-based**: Algorithms categorized by approach type (swarm intelligence, evolutionary, physics-based, AI-based, hybrid)
  3. **Formulation-based**: Problems categorized by number of objective functions (2 to 6)
- **Validation**: The classification is supported by three corresponding tables (Tables 3-5) that map references to categories.

## E03: Comparative Analysis Framework
- **Purpose**: To compare and contrast optimization approaches across multiple dimensions.
- **Methodology**: The paper develops a 14-feature comparison framework (Table 1) distinguishing classical from intelligent optimization techniques across dimensions including: method exactness, mathematical model type, intricacy handling, result type (single vs. multiple solutions), speed, global search capability, computation time, convergence behavior, solution quality, constraint handling, numerical stability, implementation difficulty, and scalability.
- **Analysis**: The comparison is qualitative, based on established characteristics of each paradigm rather than empirical benchmarking.

## E04: Trend and Gap Identification
- **Purpose**: To identify temporal trends, research concentrations, and gaps in the MOOPF literature.
- **Methodology**: Temporal analysis maps publications by year and application domain to reveal the evolution of research focus. Gap analysis identifies underexplored areas including: scalability to large grids, real-world validation, higher-dimensional objectives, comprehensive uncertainty handling, reliability/sustainability metrics, and cybersecurity considerations.

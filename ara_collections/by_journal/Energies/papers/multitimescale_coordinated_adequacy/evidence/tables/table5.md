# Table 5: Generalized Adequacy Indicators -- Planning Results (Maximum Demand Basis)

**Source:** Page 22, Section 4.3, Yin et al. 2025.
**Caption:** "Partial results of multi-type resource planning."

## Data

| Indicator | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expected Loss of Load Hours (h) | 6.15 | 4.68 | 3.14 | 2.91 |
| Expected Energy Not Supplied (MWh) | 1175.31 | 940.78 | 602.28 | 291.48 |
| Conditional Value at Risk of Expected Energy Not Supplied (MWh) | 7987.50 | 6783.61 | 1512.18 | 1316.64 |
| Flexible Ramp Capacity Margin (MW) | -356.34 | -432.78 | -600.62 | -441.81 |
| Flexible Ramp Rate Margin (MW/h) | 9.21 | -50.23 | -285.17 | -126.36 |
| System Inertia Margin (%) | -19.25 | -17.31 | 2.90 | 10.26 |
| Minimum Inertia Requirement (MW-s) | 1.46 x 10^4 | 1.45 x 10^4 | 1.52 x 10^4 | 1.54 x 10^4 |

## Notes

- Negative flexible ramp capacity/rate margin indicates sufficient ramping capability.
- Positive flexible ramp rate margin (M1: 9.21 MW/h) indicates insufficient ramping rate, posing operational risk.
- Negative inertia margin (M1, M2) indicates frequency insecurity.
- Inertia margin becomes positive only in M3 (+2.90%) and M4 (+10.26%) where frequency constraints are enforced.
- LOLH declines progressively: 6.15 -> 4.68 -> 3.14 -> 2.91, with M4 approaching the European 3 h/a standard.
- The M3 LOLH (3.14 h) still slightly exceeds the 3 h/a standard, which M4 (2.91 h) meets.
- Minimum inertia requirement slightly increases from M1 to M4 due to changing generation mix.

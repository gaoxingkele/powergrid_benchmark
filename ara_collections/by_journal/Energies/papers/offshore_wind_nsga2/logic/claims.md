# Claims

## C01: NSGA-II effectively balances investment cost and power fluctuation
- **Statement**: NSGA-II, through its fast non-dominated sorting, crowding distance, and explicit elite strategy, effectively balances the trade-off between energy storage investment cost and wind power output fluctuation in offshore wind farms.
- **Evidence**: Pareto frontier generated by NSGA-II (Figure 3, Figure 11) demonstrates clear trade-off curves; NSGA-II achieves lower xi (incremental cost per 1% volatility reduction) than MOPSO across comparable cost ranges (Table 5 vs. Table 6).
- **Reproducibility**: Algorithm parameters specified (population=20, offspring=60, generations=300, crossover/mutation probability=0.5); Python 3.8 with NSGA-II implementation via SYS module.
- **Boundary conditions**: Demonstrated for a single 40 MW wind farm with specific cost parameters from Chinese market; results are case-specific.

## C02: Energy storage battery life is primarily affected by rated capacity (positive correlation)
- **Statement**: The service life of energy storage batteries is mainly determined by rated capacity, not rated power. Larger rated capacity correlates with longer battery life, while smaller capacity leads to more frequent cycling and accelerated degradation.
- **Evidence**: Multiple linear regression model Y = 3.73292 - 0.05076*P_es + 0.22835*S_es with R^2 = 0.96965 (Equation 14, Section 4). Table 3 shows increasing expected service life from 4.1 years (4 MW/4 MWh) to 12 years (10 MW/40 MWh).
- **Reproducibility**: Regression model and data provided; cycle counts per configuration listed in Table 3.
- **Boundary conditions**: Model fitted to data from one wind farm's December week; assumes no battery performance degradation over lifecycle.

## C03: Optimal configuration under pure cost-volatility consideration is 4 MW / 28 MWh
- **Statement**: When only investment cost and power fluctuation are considered (Scheme 1), the optimal energy storage configuration is rated power of 4 MW and rated capacity of 28 MWh, yielding investment cost of CNY 349.31 million and volatility of 18.92%.
- **Evidence**: Determined by inflection point method on NSGA-II Pareto frontier (Table 7, Figure 11). This configuration achieves volatility decline rate lambda = 14.84%.
- **Reproducibility**: Ideal point and inflection point methods specified (Equations 16-19).
- **Boundary conditions**: Valid for the specific cost parameters in Table 2 and a 25-year project lifespan with two battery replacements.

## C04: Optimal configuration under spot market participation is 8 MW / 37 MWh
- **Statement**: When considering energy storage participation in the electricity spot market (Scheme 2), the optimal configuration is rated power of 8 MW and rated capacity of 37 MWh, with investment cost of CNY 242.77 million and annual volatility of 17.84%.
- **Evidence**: Determined by ideal point method on Scheme 2 Pareto frontier (Table 8, Figure 14). Spot market revenue significantly reduces net cost.
- **Reproducibility**: Uses 2024 Guangdong Province spot electricity price data (Figure 13).
- **Boundary conditions**: Based on 2024 Guangdong spot prices; results may differ for other markets or years. Inflection point method was unsuitable as no clear inflection point existed.

## C05: NSGA-II outperforms MOPSO in this multi-objective optimization problem
- **Statement**: NSGA-II produces Pareto frontiers with lower xi values (incremental cost per 1% volatility reduction) compared to MOPSO, indicating a more favorable trade-off between cost and volatility suppression.
- **Evidence**: Comparison of Tables 5 and 6 shows NSGA-II achieves lower xi at comparable investment levels. Table 7 shows NSGA-II-selected point (4 MW/28 MWh) achieves 14.84% volatility decline rate vs. MOPSO (4 MW/10 MWh) achieving 7.48%.
- **Reproducibility**: MOPSO parameters specified in Table 4; same 300 generations used.
- **Boundary conditions**: Comparison specific to this problem structure and parameter settings; MOPSO may perform differently with different parameter tuning.

## C06: Peak-valley arbitrage (Scheme 3) provides additional economic benefit over simple spot market trading (Scheme 2)
- **Statement**: Charging storage batteries only during low-price periods and discharging only during high-price periods (Scheme 3) yields additional revenue compared to Scheme 2, with the same configuration points available.
- **Evidence**: At the shared configuration point (10 MW/26 MWh), Scheme 3 investment cost is CNY 2.73 million lower than Scheme 2 (Table 8). Scheme 3 can gain additional CNY 2.73 million/year through peak-valley arbitrage.
- **Reproducibility**: Strategy defined: charge 3:00-5:00 AM (low price), discharge 6:00-8:00 PM (high price), based on Figure 13 spot price pattern.
- **Boundary conditions**: Dependent on specific spot price patterns; assumes predictable daily price cycles.

## C07: Battery life correction significantly alters the Pareto frontier
- **Statement**: Failing to account for battery life variation with rated power and capacity leads to biased economic evaluation. Life correction using multiple linear regression substantially changes the Pareto frontier and the optimal configuration decision.
- **Evidence**: Comparison of uncorrected Pareto frontier (Figure 3) vs. life-corrected Pareto frontier (Figure 6). Smaller capacity configurations have higher actual cost than NSGA-II simulated due to shorter battery life requiring more frequent replacement.
- **Reproducibility**: Life correction method described; regression equation provided (Equation 14).
- **Boundary conditions**: Regression model assumes linear relationship; actual battery degradation may follow non-linear patterns not captured.

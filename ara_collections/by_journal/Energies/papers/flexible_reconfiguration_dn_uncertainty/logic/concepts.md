# Concepts

## C01. Dynamic Reconfiguration (DR)
The process of changing the topological structure of a distribution network by opening and closing switches (sectional and tie-line) on an hourly or multi-period basis to optimize operational objectives. Unlike static reconfiguration (SR), which fixes the topology for the entire operating period, DR adapts to time-varying load and generation conditions. The paper implements complete DR where all 24 hourly periods are optimized simultaneously.

## C02. Coati Optimization Algorithm (COA)
A metaheuristic optimization algorithm inspired by the natural behavior of coatis (Nasua nasua). The algorithm simulates two behaviors: (1) hunting iguanas, where the coati population divides into two groups (one climbs to scare the iguana, the other waits on the ground), representing global exploration; and (2) escaping from predators, where coatis move to safe positions near their current location, representing local exploitation. COA is applied to the DR problem as the primary solution method.

## C03. Scenario-Based Uncertainty Modeling
A probabilistic approach that represents uncertain quantities (wind speed, solar irradiance, load demand) through discretized probability distributions. Each uncertain variable is divided into finite intervals (scenarios) with associated probabilities. Combined scenarios are formed by multiplying individual scenario probabilities, assuming independence. The paper uses 3 load scenarios, 5 wind scenarios, and 3 PV scenarios per time segment, yielding 45 combined scenarios per hour.

## C04. Remote-Controlled Switches (RCSs)
Automated switching devices installed in distribution networks that can be operated remotely without manual intervention. RCSs enable dynamic reconfiguration by allowing the system operator to change network topology at any time. The paper models RCS status as binary decision variables (1 = closed, 0 = open) and constrains each RCS to a maximum of 4 switching operations per 24-hour period.

## C05. Expected Energy Not Supplied (EENS)
A reliability metric that quantifies the expected amount of energy (in MWh/year) that cannot be delivered to consumers due to unexpected outage events. EENS is calculated using branch failure rates, average failure durations, and the load at each bus. The paper uses EENS to evaluate the reliability impact of different reconfiguration strategies.

## C06. Four-Case Comparative Framework
The paper's evaluation methodology comparing four operating scenarios: Case 1 (initial configuration without reconfiguration or DG), Case 2 (static reconfiguration for maximum load without DG), Case 3 (static reconfiguration with nominal DG output), and Case 4 (proposed dynamic reconfiguration considering uncertainty). This framework isolates the effects of reconfiguration type and DG integration.

## C07. Voltage Deviation Cost (CVD)
A penalty cost term in the objective function that quantifies the economic impact of bus voltages deviating from the rated value (1.0 p.u.). It is calculated as the sum of squared voltage deviations multiplied by a penalty factor (lambda_VD = 0.72 USD/p.u.), incentivizing the optimization to maintain voltages near the nominal value.

## C08. Radial Topology Constraint
An operational constraint requiring that the distribution network maintain a radial (tree) structure at all times, with no closed loops. The constraint is enforced by Equation (35): Nb,t = NBt - Nsub, where the number of closed branches equals the number of buses minus the number of substations. This constraint ensures proper protection coordination and operational safety.

## C09. Weibull Probability Distribution (Wind)
The statistical distribution used to model wind speed variability. The two-parameter Weibull distribution is defined by shape parameter k and scale parameter lambda, which are derived from historical wind speed data using the mean and standard deviation. Wind speed scenarios are generated by integrating the Weibull PDF over discrete intervals, and wind-DG power output is computed using the turbine power curve (Equation 6).

## C10. Beta Probability Distribution (Solar)
The statistical distribution used to model solar irradiance variability. The Beta PDF is defined by shape parameters alpha and beta, calculated from the mean and standard deviation of historical irradiance data. Solar irradiance scenarios feed into the PV array model (Equations 12-16) that accounts for cell temperature, fill factor, and module characteristics to compute PV power output.

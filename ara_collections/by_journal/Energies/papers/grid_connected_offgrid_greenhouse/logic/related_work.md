# Related Work

## Positioning
This study contributes to the literature on hybrid renewable energy system (HRES) optimization for agricultural applications. It is distinguished by its systematic comparison of multiple hybrid architectures (16 total configurations across grid-connected and off-grid scenarios) under consistent technical and economic assumptions, addressing a gap where prior work typically analyzed only 1-2 configurations in isolation.

## Key Related Studies

### Grid-Connected Large-Scale Wind/PV Optimization
Studies [37-39] (Seedahmed et al., Dalton et al.) focused on optimizing grid-connected wind and PV systems, finding wind energy can be more cost-effective than PV in suitable locations. This study finds the opposite (PV outperforms wind at the Sandikli site), highlighting site-specificity.

### Solar-Diesel Hybrid Systems for Rural Areas
Wahid et al. [41] and others concluded solar-diesel hybrid systems are more sustainable for rural areas. This study supports this finding with the optimal Gen/PV/B configuration.

### Generator + Hybrid Cost Comparisons
Dincsoy [42] found that replacing grid with generators in wind/solar hybrids increases initial investment 4-5x and extends payback 5-8x. This study confirms this pattern.

### Southern Ghana Hybrid Feasibility
Studies [43-45] (Himri et al., Ozcan, Adaramola) assessed solar/wind/diesel hybrid feasibility in Ghana, identifying optimal configurations based on LCOE and NPC. This study applies the same methodology to the Turkiye greenhouse context.

### Rural Malaysia Wind-Solar Hybrids
Ngan and Tan [46], Suresh et al. [47], and Olatomiwa et al. [48] found standalone wind-solar hybrids are more stable and economical than single-source systems. Partially supported: G/PV outperforms G/WT at this site.

### PV-Diesel-Battery Hybrids in Agriculture
Woldegiyorgis et al. [101], Samatar et al. [102], and Eze et al. [103] found PV-diesel-battery hybrids provide the most practical compromise between cost and emissions in rural agricultural contexts. This study's Gen/PV/B finding is directly aligned.

### Fully Renewable Systems
Ferreira et al. [26], Eze et al. [103], and Kumar et al. [104] found that while zero-emission systems are technologically feasible, adoption depends on cost reductions in battery and wind turbine technologies. This study reinforces this conclusion.

### Battery Degradation and Sensitivity Gap
The study claims prior work [68-76] often omits battery degradation effects and provides limited sensitivity analysis on solar radiation, wind, and fuel price uncertainties. This study claims to address this gap through comprehensive sensitivity analysis.

## Research Gap Addressed
1. **Systematic multi-configuration comparison**: Prior work analyzed isolated configurations; this study compares 16 architectures under uniform assumptions.
2. **Comprehensive sensitivity analysis**: Includes inflation, fuel prices, and renewable resource variation.
3. **Agricultural greenhouse focus**: Specific high-demand facility with detailed load profile.
4. **Grid-connected and off-grid comparison**: Dual analysis under consistent assumptions.

# Proposed DSS Method: Eight-Stage Architecture

## Overview

The proposed Decision Support System (DSS) is a secondary-data-driven framework with AI-assisted criterion documentation and a fuzzy-interval extension. Its purpose is to rank countries according to strategic energy investment readiness, explain how rankings are formed, show how they change across stakeholder profiles, and demonstrate how uncertainty can be represented when sufficient historical data are available.

## Stage 1: LLM-Assisted Criterion Extraction

Policy and strategy documents are used as a knowledge base for identifying energy investment criteria. A large language model (LLM)-assisted extraction layer identifies recurring decision dimensions and terminology. The LLM is NOT used as an autonomous decision maker -- candidate criteria are retained only after research-team review confirming:
- Relevance to strategic energy investment readiness
- Public-data availability and cross-country comparability
- Clear benefit/cost direction
- Non-duplication

## Stage 2: Secondary-Data Decision Matrix Construction

Extracted criteria are matched with measurable secondary indicators from public data sources (World Bank WDI/WGI, OWID/Ember, IRENA, NASA POWER, Eurostat, ENTSO-E, Countryeconomy) to construct a country-level decision matrix X = [x_ij] where i indexes countries and j indexes criteria.

**Empirical specification**: 36 countries x 18 criteria grouped into 6 dimensions:
1. Macroeconomic feasibility (C01-C04: GDP per capita, GDP growth, inflation, FDI inflows)
2. Institutional capacity (C05-C06: regulatory quality, government effectiveness)
3. Energy security (C07-C09: energy import dependence, fossil electricity share, net electricity imports)
4. Sustainability and decarbonization (C10-C12, C17: renewable electricity share, renewable capacity, electricity carbon intensity, CO2 per capita)
5. Market and demand conditions (C13-C16: electricity demand, demand growth, price volatility)
6. Technical resource potential (C17-C18: solar irradiance, wind speed)

## Stage 3: Normalization

Raw data are transformed into comparable values through:
- **Benefit/cost classification**: Benefit (higher = better) or cost (higher = worse) per criterion
- **Min-max normalization**: Benefit: r_ij = (x_ij - min(x_j)) / (max(x_j) - min(x_j)); Cost: r_ij = (max(x_j) - x_ij) / (max(x_j) - min(x_j))

All normalized values range in [0,1] with higher always indicating stronger readiness.

## Stage 4: Fuzzy-Interval Extension (Modular)

When sufficient time-series data are available, normalized values can be represented as triangular fuzzy numbers with lower, modal, and upper bounds derived from empirical percentiles, volatility bands, or scenario ranges. In the empirical baseline, the latest available normalized values are used (fuzzy treated as extension).

## Stage 5: Weighting and Scoring

**Empirically activated methods:**
- Equal-weight baseline (w_j = 1/m)
- Entropy objective weighting (based on information dispersion)
- CRITIC objective weighting (based on variability + inter-criterion conflict)
- Hybrid entropy-CRITIC weighting (arithmetic mean)

**Modular extensions** (not activated in baseline):
- Causal-evidence weighting
- LLM-assisted salience weighting

**Scoring**: Baseline readiness score S_i = sum_j w_j * r_ij; fuzzy extension uses centroid defuzzification.

## Stage 6: Persona-Based Personalized Scoring

Stakeholder-specific weight vectors W^(p) are defined for five personas: public planner, private investor, grid operator, sustainability policymaker, infrastructure fund. Persona-specific score: S_i^(p) = sum_j w_j^(p) * r_ij.

## Stage 7: Explainability and Fairness Layer

**Explainability**: Criterion-level contribution C_ij = w_j * r_ij decomposes each score into per-criterion drivers. Aggregation by dimension shows which readiness areas drive or constrain each country's position.

**Fairness**: Distributional diagnostics examine whether rankings systematically favor high-income countries, institutionally mature countries, or countries with existing renewable infrastructure. Partial-correlation analysis controls for GDP and institutions to assess residual contribution of other dimensions.

## Stage 8: Simulated-Agent Validation Laboratory

500 synthetic decision agents are generated by varying dimension importance weights around stakeholder-relevant anchors. Rankings from each agent are compared with baseline using Spearman correlation, top-k overlap, and largest rank change metrics. This tests whether ranking conclusions remain stable under heterogeneous decision preferences.

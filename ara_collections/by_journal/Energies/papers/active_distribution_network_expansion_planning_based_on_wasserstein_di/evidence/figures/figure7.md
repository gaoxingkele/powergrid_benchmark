## Visual description
- **Components**: Grouped bar chart comparing Case 1 (lines + SOPs + interconnection switches) and Case 4 (Case 1 with all SOPs replaced by interconnection switches) across three operation indexes: DG accommodation rate (%), average line load rate (%), and network loss (kW).
- **Axes**:
  - X-axis: three operation index categories grouped by case.
  - Y-axis: percentage (for DG accommodation rate and line load rate) or power (kW, for network loss).
- **Data values (estimated from chart)**:
  - DG Accommodation Rate: Case 1 ~99.5%, Case 4 ~93.2% (Case 4 experiences more DG curtailment due to lack of flexible regulation).
  - Average Line Load Rate: Case 1 ~42%, Case 4 ~38% (Case 4 has lower utilization due to inflexible switching).
  - Network Loss: Case 1 ~520 kW, Case 4 ~650 kW (Case 4 has higher network losses due to lack of continuous reactive power control).
- **What it conveys**: Replacing SOPs with interconnection switches (Case 4) degrades all three operation indexes: DG accommodation rate decreases (more renewable curtailment), average line load rate decreases (less efficient utilization), and network losses increase. This supports the economic advantage of power-electronic SOPs over mechanical interconnection switches for flexible distribution network operation.

Supporting data also in Table 5 (Section 5.3): Case 4 annual operating income is 4807.155 x 10^4 CNY/year, reduced by more than 6% vs Case 1 (5097.78 x 10^4 CNY/year).

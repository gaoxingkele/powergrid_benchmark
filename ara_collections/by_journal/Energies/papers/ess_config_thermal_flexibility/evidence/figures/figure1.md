## Visual description
- **Components**: The diagram shows three main blocks connected by arrows:
  1. "Power grid" / "Distribution system" — central block representing the electrical distribution network
  2. "ESS" / "Energy storage station" — a dedicated block with a "control center" sub-block that manages scheduling instructions
  3. "Controllable building loads" — multiple building icons representing loads that can be powered by PV, wind power, ESS, and the grid; their energy primarily originates from conventional electrical loads and air-conditioning loads
- **Connections**: Bidirectional arrows between ESS and the distribution system (power flow in both directions). Unidirectional arrows from the distribution system to the controllable building loads (power delivery). Arrow from ESS control center to building loads (scheduling/dispatch instructions).
- **Annotations**: The diagram includes three energy sources feeding into the distribution system: photovoltaic, wind power, and the external grid. The control center inside the ESS block manages "charging and discharging requirements of controllable building loads in each time period."
- **What it conveys**: The ESS operates as an intermediary between the grid and building loads, with a control center that determines ESS capacity and peak charging/discharging power by analyzing the cyclic behavior of all controllable building loads, and administers user payments aligned with time-of-use tariffs.

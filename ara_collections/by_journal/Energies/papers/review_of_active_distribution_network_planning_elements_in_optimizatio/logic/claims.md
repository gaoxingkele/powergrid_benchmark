# Claims

This review paper synthesizes findings about the landscape of active distribution network planning. Claims describe what the review finds, not experimental outcomes.

## Claim 1: Passive-to-active transition taxonomy

**Statement:** The review finds that the transition from passive to active distribution network planning requires evolution across five dimensions: planning horizons (short/long/horizon-year), objectives (economic/technical/environmental), decision variables (traditional infrastructure + flexible sources), constraints (technical/non-technical/investment/time), and uncertainty modeling (probabilistic/possibilistic/hybrid/IGDT/Monte Carlo/analytical).

**Proof:** Review of Sections 2.1–2.6, Tables 1–8, Figures 1–3.
**Experiment IDs:** E1, E2

## Claim 2: OPF formulation taxonomy

**Statement:** The review classifies OPF formulations for ADN planning into five categories — Linear Programming, Mixed-Integer Linear Programming, Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming, Convex Relaxations (SOC), and Metaheuristic Methods — each with distinct accuracy-computational trade-offs characterized in the literature.

**Proof:** Section 3, Table 9.
**Experiment IDs:** E1, E2

## Claim 3: Generative AI application landscape

**Statement:** The review finds that generative AI models applied to ADN planning fall into five families — GAN-based, diffusion-based, VAE-based, flow-based, and transformer-based — with distinct strengths: GANs for realistic data generation, diffusion models for extreme event modeling, VAEs for probabilistic representations, flow-based models for exact likelihood estimation, and transformers for reasoning and decision support.

**Proof:** Section 4, Table 10, Figure 10, references [63–101].
**Experiment IDs:** E1, E3

## Claim 4: Inadequacy of traditional planning

**Statement:** The review identifies that traditional distribution planning — characterized by passive "fit and forget" reinforcement, worst-case assumptions, and deterministic methods — is inadequate for networks with high DER penetration, EV integration, and flexible loads that require AC-OPF formulations, new asset modeling, and dynamic optimization.

**Proof:** Sections 1 and 5, Table 1, Figures 1–2.
**Experiment IDs:** E1, E2

## Claim 5: Emerging asset integration risks

**Statement:** The review reveals that active distribution planning faces critical challenges from accelerating integration of data centers, AI factories, renewable energy communities, and fast-charging hubs, which introduce high-magnitude, fast-ramping events outside traditional grid design parameters.

**Proof:** Section 5, Conclusions.
**Experiment IDs:** E1

## Claim 6: GenAI for scenario generation dominance

**Statement:** The review finds that GAN-based and diffusion-based models are the most prevalent generative AI approaches for scenario generation in ADN planning, with transformer-based models dominating planning assistance and decision support applications.

**Proof:** Section 4, Figure 10 (Sankey diagram), Table 10.
**Experiment IDs:** E1, E3

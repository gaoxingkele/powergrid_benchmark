## C²GES

Title: *Causal and Counterfactual Graph-Enhanced Extractive Summarization
(C²GES) for Power Grid Maintenance Reports*

### Abstract (one paragraph, 200--250 words)

Six moves: operational problem; evidence gap; implemented typed causal graph;
deterministic structural counterfactual intervention; constrained extractive
selection; report-level results and limitations.  The former DOCX values
41.1/18.9/37.6, 68.2, and 71.5 are prohibited.  Report only values generated by
the new NERC runner, and call the references official executive-summary text,
not domain-expert annotation.

### 1. Introduction (6--8 paragraphs)

1. Maintenance/disturbance-report overload and the need for traceable extracts.
2. Why lexical/semantic salience can omit cause--propagation--impact--mitigation.
3. Difference between textual causal proxies and physical/structural causality.
4. Research questions: summary quality, causal-function coverage, incremental
   value of graph and intervention terms, and stability across reports.
5. Method overview, scoped to typed sentence-level proxy graphs.
6. Contributions tied separately to implementation, benchmark construction,
   experiment, and auditability.
7. Evidence boundary: 21 eligible official reports in the current deterministic
   build, machine-silver auxiliary roles, no human-expert gold claim.

### 2. Related Work (4 subsections, 2--4 paragraphs each)

- Extractive and query-focused summarization.
- Graph-based sentence ranking and graph neural summarization.  Do not claim a
  GNN contribution unless an executed GNN implementation and checkpoint enter
  the frozen experiment.
- Causal/counterfactual NLP, distinguishing intervention on a proxy graph from
  identification of real-world causal effects.
- Power-grid/engineering-document NLP and nearest Applied Sciences work.

### 3. Task, Data, and Evidence Boundary

- Define a report as candidate sentences plus an official executive-summary
  reference where available.
- Give deterministic inclusion/exclusion, dev/test split, hashes, and the exact
  current denominator.
- Describe FEVER only as an auxiliary evidence-selection benchmark.
- Define machine-silver role data and prohibit expert-ground-truth language.
- State licensing and editor/reviewer access terms.

### 4. Method

- Base relevance and role channels inherited from the earlier asset family.
- `CausalEventGraph`: sentence nodes, typed directed proxy edges, construction
  rules, serialization, and failure cases.
- Structural interventions: remove one registered node/edge, recompute typed
  flow, and define counterfactual sensitivity.
- Scoring equation with relevance, role, graph, intervention, position, and
  redundancy terms.  Every term must map to executable code.
- Constrained selection: causal-function coverage, budget, redundancy penalty,
  and restoration of report order.
- Complexity and reproducibility.

### 5. Experimental Design

- Conditions: lead, centroid, TextRank, role, graph-without-intervention, full.
- Same candidate sentences and summary budget for every paired report.
- Metrics: ROUGE-1/2/L, causal-function coverage, redundancy, length, runtime.
- Paired report-cluster bootstrap and a prespecified primary contrast.
- Auxiliary FEVER/BGE/MiniLM evidence retained in a clearly separated subsection.
- Ablations and qualitative case selection fixed before inspecting test results.

### 6. Results

- Data-flow and exclusion audit first.
- Main report-level table with effect differences and intervals.
- Ablation and per-report stability.
- Auxiliary FEVER findings, preserving the existing negative role-effect result.
- One fully traceable report case with sentence IDs, graph edges, interventions,
  and selected summary.

### 7. Discussion and Limitations

Interpret practical effect sizes before p-values.  Discuss small report count,
official-summary/reference mismatch, proxy causal graph, absence of human graph
gold, deterministic perturbations, and lack of evidence for physical causal
discovery or operational deployment.

### 8. Conclusions

Answer each research question without superiority language unless the frozen
results support it.

## MA-SQLGrid

Title: *MA-SQLGrid: A Robust Multi-Agent Framework for Text-to-SQL in Power Grid
Databases*

### Abstract (one paragraph, 200--250 words)

State the database-access problem, five implemented roles, append-only
blackboard, execution/counterfactual evidence, deterministic adjudication and
abstention, inherited GridDB/BIRD evidence, and the status of the new
coordination experiment.  The former DOCX Spider/WikiSQL/GridDB values and
robustness drops are prohibited.

### 1. Introduction (6--8 paragraphs)

1. Natural-language access to power-grid databases.
2. Schema complexity, semantic error, and read-only safety.
3. Why a one-call or multi-stage pipeline is not automatically multi-agent.
4. Research questions about coordination efficacy, validation, robustness,
   abstention, and cost.
5. Five-role framework and deterministic evidence boundary.
6. Contributions separated into architecture, audit protocol, experiment, and
   application evidence.
7. Boundary: no deployment or operator validation claim.

### 2. Related Work

- Text-to-SQL and cross-database evaluation.
- Schema grounding, execution guidance, and repair.
- LLM multi-agent coordination, voting, and adjudication.
- Power-grid database applications and DKA-SQL nearest-work boundary.

### 3. Task and Data

- Define question, schema, database, candidate SQL, blackboard, decision, and
  gold-isolated offline evaluator.
- Correct GridDB facts: 200 records (20 development, 180 evaluation), not 1000
  expert-authored pairs.
- BIRD 500 items/11 databases and inherited 5000-call protocol.
- RTS-GMLC and SimBench only as automatic portability diagnostics.
- Data licensing and reviewer-access statement.

### 4. MA-SQLGrid Framework

- Query Analyst and typed intent contract.
- Schema Cartographer and schema/value grounding contract.
- SQL Synthesizer and ordered, deduplicated candidate contract.
- Execution/Safety Validator.
- Counterfactual Critic over registered perturbation/state evidence.
- Append-only blackboard with message hashes.
- Deterministic adjudicator, tie-break, stop rule, and abstention.
- Explicitly separate implemented coordination from inherited one-call results.

### 5. Experimental Design

- Inherited studies: GridDB factorial, prospective components, BIRD and 15-state
  reliability; preserve original labels and denominators.
- New conditions: direct, multi-stage, candidate pool without critic, no
  validation, voting/adjudication, and full framework.
- Two frozen backbones, paired questions, fixed call/order/retry budget.
- Execution accuracy/test-suite accuracy, valid/safe rate, rescue/harm,
  abstention, counterfactual consistency, tokens and latency.
- Clustered randomization/bootstrap and family-wise correction.
- Retrospective offline replay is diagnostic and may not replace the prospective
  coordination comparison.

### 6. Results

- Inherited evidence table with negative factorial findings intact.
- New coordination main table only after an authorized frozen run.
- Validator/critic/adjudicator ablations.
- Failure taxonomy, abstention, and cost.
- One ledger-derived case study with all agent messages and hashes.

### 7. Discussion and Limitations

Discuss snapshot sensitivity, synthetic GridDB size, exposed development data,
execution-equality limitations, retrospective replay bias, automatic external
candidates, missing operator validation, and cost/latency trade-offs.

### 8. Conclusions

Conclude on auditability and bounded robustness, not general industrial
reliability or universal multi-agent superiority.

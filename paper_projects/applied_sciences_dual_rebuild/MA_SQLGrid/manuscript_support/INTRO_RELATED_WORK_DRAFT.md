## 1. Introduction

Relational databases remain a central interface for operational records, but their query language is inaccessible to many domain specialists. Text-to-SQL addresses this interface problem by translating a natural-language request into executable SQL over a supplied database. The field has progressed from single-domain settings to cross-domain benchmarks and, more recently, to large databases and enterprise workflows; surveys nevertheless emphasize that successful generation requires coordinated treatment of question semantics, schema structure, database values, and decoding constraints rather than fluent SQL text alone \cite{katsogiannis2023survey,yu2018spider,li2023bird,lei2025spider2}. <!-- LIT-01, LIT-02 -->

This challenge is particularly consequential in power-grid maintenance and operational data. An apparently plausible query can select a wrong status code, follow an invalid join path, omit a requested grouping, or return the wrong projection while still looking syntactically credible. Public grid resources such as RTS-GMLC and SimBench provide versioned, structured power-system data that support reproducible application studies, but they were designed as power-system benchmarks rather than natural-language-to-SQL gold corpora \cite{barrows2020rts,meinecke2020simbench}. Applied power-grid NLP studies also show that domain terminology and relations require explicit treatment, although relation extraction is a different task from executable database querying \cite{meng2023gridroberta}. <!-- LIT-03, LIT-04 -->

Schema grounding is one persistent source of difficulty. Spider made generalization to unseen schemas an explicit benchmark objective, and RAT-SQL represented relations between questions, tables, and columns directly \cite{yu2018spider,wang2020ratsql}. Subsequent analyses found that schema-linking decisions materially affect text-to-SQL behavior, while graph-based and path-finding approaches made the link structure more explicit \cite{lei2020reexamining,liu2022semantic,safdarian2026schemagraphsql}. At LLM scale, CHESS similarly retrieves database content and prunes large catalogs before synthesis. These systems motivate selective context, but their end-to-end pipelines do not by themselves establish that compact schema context has a positive effect independently of other prompt or validation components \cite{talaei2024chess}. <!-- LIT-05 -->

Prompt design introduces a second, partially confounded control surface. Decomposition, tailored schema-linking prompts, clause-level guidance, and execution-consistency selection have all improved text-to-SQL systems in their reported settings \cite{pourreza2023dinsql,nan2023enhancing,sun2023sqlprompt,tan2024tailored}. However, a prompt may improve the form of an answer without improving its denotation. In an operational interface, projection count, column identity, aggregation, ordering, and null behavior are part of the answer contract; they should therefore be measured separately from execution correctness rather than treated as interchangeable evidence. <!-- LIT-06, LIT-07 -->

Evaluation design is consequently part of the method. Exact string or structural match can reject semantically equivalent SQL, whereas execution on a single database instance can fail to distinguish nonequivalent queries. Distilled test suites were proposed to approximate semantic accuracy across multiple generated database instances, and realistic benchmarks have exposed degradation under domain, schema, and language variation \cite{zhong2020semantic,lee2021kaggledbqa,gan2021robustness}. These findings support reporting a frozen execution contract, paired comparisons, and explicit metric sensitivity. They also caution against interpreting output-format compliance as content retrieval. <!-- LIT-08 -->

Post-generation control provides another route to reliability. PICARD rejects inadmissible tokens during decoding; error-correction work edits clause-level query structures; SQLPrompt selects candidates using execution consistency; and DART-SQL combines database content with execution-guided refinement \cite{scholak2021picard,chen2023texttosql,sun2023sqlprompt,mao2024dartsql}. Such mechanisms can reduce syntactic or execution failures, but a reference-free validator cannot guarantee that an executable query expresses the intended domain semantics. We therefore separate generation, safety/executability checks, semantic scoring, and any future repair analysis. <!-- LIT-09 -->

The closest same-journal technical precedent is DKASQL, published in *Applied Sciences* in 2025. DKASQL combines domain-knowledge extraction, SQL generation, verification loops, and a knowledge memory, and evaluates both BIRD and the power-grid supply-chain ElecSQL dataset \cite{bian2025dkasql}. MA-SQLGrid does not claim to be the first domain-specific or power-grid Text-to-SQL framework. Its distinct question is narrower and experimental: when the generator and question are held fixed, what are the separate and interacting effects of schema scope and answer-shape guidance, and do those effects retain the same direction and magnitude across two local quantized instruction backbones? <!-- LIT-10 -->

To answer that question, we formulate MA-SQLGrid as a paired (2\times2) factorial study over 180 GridDB questions. The factors are full versus compact schema context and absence versus presence of an answer-shape hint. Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct Q4_K_M and Granite-3.3-8B-Instruct Q4_K_M each contribute a complete 720-row Cartesian run, yielding 1440 canonical predictions evaluated under the same question, prompt, data, and scoring boundaries. Question-level pairing and normalized-gold-SQL template clusters are preserved for bootstrap inference; prespecified binary contrasts use exact McNemar tests with Holm adjustment. <!-- MA-C10, MA-C11, MA-C15 | local evidence: ../canonical_dual_backbone/release_manifest.json; ../canonical_dual_backbone/tables/table01_dual_cell_accuracy.csv -->

The study makes four bounded contributions. First, it supplies an auditable factorial protocol that separates context scope from response-contract guidance instead of attributing a joint pipeline change to either component. Second, it shows that shape-hint point estimates are positive for execution on both tested backbones, while the Granite interval includes zero; answer-shape effects exclude zero for both. Third, it identifies backbone sensitivity in effect magnitude and in the context-by-shape interaction, rejecting a universal compact-context recipe. Fourth, it releases mechanical portability pilots for RTS-GMLC and SimBench while withholding any cross-database accuracy claim until human review is completed. <!-- MA-C01, MA-C02, MA-C03 E4-NO-GO, MA-C13, MA-C14 | local evidence: ../canonical_dual_backbone/tables/table02_backbone_factorial_effects.csv; ../canonical_dual_backbone/tables/table03_backbone_effect_modifiers.csv; ../external_protocol/W4_MA_EXTERNAL_PROTOCOL_REPORT.md -->

The evidence boundary is deliberate. The main results cover two quantized instruction backbones, one synthetic maintenance database, one execution per backbone, and one frozen factorial prompt design. RTS-GMLC and SimBench are automatically constructed, development-visible pilots rather than human-gold Text-to-SQL benchmarks; latency, energy, memory, validator-repair benefit, and cross-database accuracy remain outside the eligible claim set. The paper therefore presents a reproducible component analysis for an applied power-grid database interface, not a general state-of-the-art ranking or a deployment-readiness claim. <!-- MA-C05 PROHIBITED, MA-C06 HUMAN-DEPENDENT, MA-C08 PENDING-E4, MA-C09 PENDING-E4 -->

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1. Text-to-SQL Tasks and Realistic Evaluation

Spider established database-disjoint evaluation across complex schemas and queries, making cross-schema generalization a central text-to-SQL problem \cite{yu2018spider}. BIRD expanded the applied setting to larger databases, database values, external knowledge, and query efficiency, while Spider 2.0 moved toward multi-dialect enterprise workflows with extensive metadata and project context \cite{li2023bird,lei2025spider2}. KaggleDBQA and synonym-substitution tests separately showed that strong benchmark performance need not transfer unchanged to naturally authored databases or linguistically perturbed questions \cite{lee2021kaggledbqa,gan2021robustness}. MA-SQLGrid is not a replacement for these benchmarks: GridDB is a controlled domain corpus for paired intervention estimation, and its external resources remain portability pilots.

### 2.2. Schema Linking, Context Selection, and Database Values

Schema linking connects question mentions to tables, columns, keys, and values. RAT-SQL encoded question-schema relations jointly, Lei et al. re-examined which linking signals account for model behavior, and IESQL learned a schema-linking graph iteratively \cite{wang2020ratsql,lei2020reexamining,liu2022semantic}. Recent SchemaGraphSQL uses foreign-key graphs and path finding to select joinable schema elements; SDE-SQL explores a database with SQL probes; and CHESS combines information retrieval with adaptive schema pruning for large catalogs \cite{safdarian2026schemagraphsql,xie2026sdesql,talaei2024chess}. BIRD further demonstrates why database values and external knowledge matter in realistic database grounding \cite{li2023bird}. MA-SQLGrid's compact/full factor belongs to this research line, but the factorial design prevents a favorable joint condition from being misreported as an independent compactness gain.

### 2.3. LLM Prompting and Decomposed Generation

LLM-based systems often divide text-to-SQL into intermediate decisions. DIN-SQL decomposes schema linking, query classification, generation, and self-correction; Nan et al. compare prompt-design strategies; SQLPrompt combines multiple prompts with execution-based consistency; and tailored prompting work separately elicits schema linking and clause construction \cite{pourreza2023dinsql,nan2023enhancing,sun2023sqlprompt,tan2024tailored}. CHESS extends decomposition into a multi-agent pipeline with retrieval, schema selection, candidate generation, and unit testing \cite{talaei2024chess}. These studies establish that context presentation and workflow structure can matter, but most compare complete systems or nested modules. MA-SQLGrid instead holds the question and generator fixed across four aligned cells so that schema scope, shape guidance, and their interaction are explicit estimands.

### 2.4. Constrained Decoding, Repair, and Semantic Scoring

Reliability interventions operate at different stages and should not be conflated. PICARD constrains token generation to prefixes admitted by an incremental SQL parser, whereas clause-level correction edits a completed candidate \cite{scholak2021picard,chen2023texttosql}. DART-SQL and SQLPrompt use execution information for refinement or candidate consistency \cite{mao2024dartsql,sun2023sqlprompt}. Evaluation then asks a separate question: whether the resulting SQL is semantically equivalent to the reference. Zhong et al. show why structural comparison and execution on limited instances can disagree and propose distilled test-suite evaluation \cite{zhong2020semantic}. MA-SQLGrid therefore treats safety, executability, answer-shape correctness, and strict execution correctness as distinct outcomes; its pending validator-repair claim is not inferred from generation accuracy.

### 2.5. Domain-Specific and Power-Grid Data Interfaces

Domain-specific text-to-SQL must reconcile schema structure with terminology, encoded values, and business rules. DKASQL is the direct technical comparator: it retrieves domain knowledge, iterates extraction/generation with verification, and reports results on BIRD and power-grid supply-chain data \cite{bian2025dkasql}. MA-SQLGrid differs in contribution rather than application label: it tests prompt-factor effects with two local quantized backbones and emphasizes paired uncertainty, interaction, and negative findings. The grid relation-extraction study by Meng et al. supports the broader observation that power-grid language requires domain-sensitive representation, but it is not a text-to-SQL baseline \cite{meng2023gridroberta}. RTS-GMLC and SimBench support reproducible grid-data pilots, yet their source papers do not provide natural-language questions or expert SQL annotations \cite{barrows2020rts,meinecke2020simbench}.

### 2.6. Position of MA-SQLGrid

The literature supports selective grounding, prompt decomposition, execution-aware control, and domain adaptation, but it also exposes a recurring inference risk: improvements from a multi-component pipeline do not identify which component caused the change. MA-SQLGrid addresses that risk with a complete paired factorial comparison and a two-backbone effect-modifier analysis. Its novelty claim is therefore not “first power-grid Text-to-SQL,” “compact context always helps,” or “verification guarantees correctness.” The defensible claim is that schema scope and answer-shape guidance have separable, backbone-sensitive effects under a frozen power-grid maintenance benchmark, and that those effects should be reported with semantic, statistical, and external-validity boundaries. <!-- MA-C03 E4-NO-GO, MA-C13, MA-C14 -->

SHIELD-MOEA draws on three bodies of literature that have so far evolved largely in parallel: resilience-oriented planning and hardening of distribution networks under N-k and extreme-weather uncertainty, scenario generation and reduction for stochastic power system optimization, and hybrid multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) with robustness-aware objectives. Below we discuss each thread, identify where their assumptions break down for scenario-aware evolutionary planning, and state the gap that motivates this paper.

## Resilience-Oriented Distribution Planning and Hardening Decisions

The conceptual vocabulary of power system resilience — distinguishing high-impact low-probability events from ordinary reliability, and hardening from operational adaptation — was consolidated by [Panteli and Mancarella, 2015], and the survey of [Bhusal et al., 2020] documents how quickly planning-stage resilience enhancement became a research field of its own. Within that field, the mathematically dominant paradigm is robust optimization over an N-k or weather-driven uncertainty set. [Yuan et al., 2016] formulated resilient distribution network planning as a two-stage robust problem in which an adversary disables up to k components; [Ma et al., 2018] extended the defender-attacker-defender logic to joint hardening and distributed-generation placement with wind-dependent fragility of overhead lines; and [Lin and Bie, 2018] added post-event reconfiguration and DG islanding inside a tri-level hardening model. On the probabilistic side, [Panteli et al., 2017] showed how fragility curves translate weather intensity into component failure probabilities, giving hardening studies a physically grounded scenario model. Recent work in our target venues carries this agenda into practice-oriented settings: distributed energy storage siting for resilience [Chen et al., 2026], differential (zone-wise) resilience planning under decision-dependent uncertainty [Chen et al., 2025], genetic-algorithm-driven hardening of distribution feeders [Maris et al., 2025], multi-criteria planning of resilient renewable microgrids [Kiptoo et al., 2023], and islanding plus fault-recovery strategies under new-energy uncertainty [Yang et al., 2023]. Reading these papers side by side reveals a consistent pattern in how uncertainty enters the decision loop. The trilevel robust models protect against a single worst case and collapse the objective space to one scalar, so the planner never sees the cost-resilience trade surface. The metaheuristic studies do expose multiple objectives, but they evaluate candidate plans on a scenario set fixed before the search starts, and — critically — the same scenarios serve both to steer the search and to grade its output. None of them guards against a plan that has quietly overfitted the scenario sample it was optimized on.

## Scenario Generation, Reduction, and Screening in Stochastic Power System Optimization

The second thread supplies the machinery for representing uncertainty compactly. Classical scenario reduction selects a small subset of a large scenario fan while minimizing a probability distance to the original distribution; the Fortet-Mourier-metric formulation of [Dupačová et al., 2003] and the forward-selection and backward-reduction algorithms of [Heitsch and Römisch, 2003] remain the reference methods. Power system applications adopted them early — [Morales et al., 2009] tailored the metric to the cost impact of scenarios in electricity-market trading — and [Papavasiliou and Oren, 2013] demonstrated that importance-sampled scenario selection makes stochastic unit commitment with high wind penetration tractable at realistic scale. On the generation side, [Chen et al., 2018] moved from parametric sampling to model-free generative adversarial networks that reproduce spatiotemporal renewable behavior. Target-venue planning papers use this toolbox in the expected way: a multi-stage stochastic MILP for renewable microgrid dispatch builds its tree from reduced scenario sets [Babatunde et al., 2025], distribution reconfiguration under renewable and load uncertainty evaluates a clustered scenario collection [Esmaeilnezhad et al., 2025], and joint siting-and-sizing under new-energy uncertainty optimizes against representative scenarios extracted before the run [Wang et al., 2025]. Two design choices are near-universal in this thread. First, reduction is executed once, offline, and judged by distributional fidelity — which scenarios are kept has nothing to do with which scenarios are hard for the solutions currently under consideration. Second, the reduced set is reused for every evaluation the optimizer performs, so the distinction between "scenarios that guide the search" and "scenarios that certify the result" does not exist. For mathematical programming with a single monolithic solve, that distinction is arguably moot; for population-based search, where thousands of candidate plans are compared across generations and the binding scenarios shift as the population improves, a static, search-agnostic scenario set is a poor fit — yet in-the-loop scenario screening for evolutionary planning is essentially absent from this literature.

## Hybrid Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms and Robustness-Aware Objectives

The third thread concerns the optimizer itself. NSGA-II [Deb et al., 2002] and MOEA/D [Zhang and Li, 2007] remain the workhorse frameworks, and hypervolume [Zitzler and Thiele, 1999] the default measure of front quality. Differential evolution [Storn and Price, 1997] contributes mutation operators whose search behavior complements GA crossover, and the survey of [Das and Suganthan, 2011] catalogs the many schemes that blend or adapt DE strategies — a lineage that motivates the GA/DE hybrid variation used in this paper. How such algorithms should cope with noisy or uncertain fitness was mapped out by [Jin and Branke, 2005], and [Deb and Gupta, 2006] made robustness itself an objective by optimizing mean-effective functions over perturbation neighborhoods. In the power-planning venues we target, hybridization is flourishing but is aimed almost exclusively at the variation and staging machinery: an improved NSGA-II for storage-and-EV distribution co-optimization [He et al., 2025], a three-stage TER-NSGA-II for resilient backbone-grid planning [Ding et al., 2026], and an enhanced beluga whale optimizer for vulnerability-driven storage planning in resilient distribution networks [Qi et al., 2025]. When these hybrids face uncertainty, they either average fitness over a fixed sample of scenarios — paying the full evaluation cost every generation and diluting selection pressure with scenario noise — or push the uncertainty into a robust reformulation solved before the evolutionary search begins. What no hybrid in this space does, to our knowledge, is treat the scenario subset itself as a search-time adaptive quantity: re-identifying, as the population evolves, the scenarios that currently hurt candidate plans the most, and concentrating fitness evaluation there. Nor does robustness reporting extend past mean front quality; a worst-case hypervolume over a certified scenario set is not part of the standard results table.

## Relation to CARS-MODE and Gap Statement

A companion algorithm from our research program, CARS-MODE [sibling], also performs evolutionary distribution-network planning on public benchmark-derived portfolios, so we state the division of labor precisely. CARS-MODE is a self-adaptive multi-objective differential evolution method: its contribution lies in steering mutation, crossover, and constraint-repair intensity from population diversity and violation feedback, and its problem setting is economic DER-and-storage integration planning under conventional operating conditions. SHIELD-MOEA takes the complementary route. Its evolutionary kernel is deliberately standard (non-dominated sorting with GA/DE hybrid variation plus feasibility repair); the novelty is placed in the interface between the optimizer and the uncertainty model — periodic screening of the currently-worst scenarios to serve as search-phase fitness, strict separation of screening scenarios from a disjoint-seed evaluation scenario set, and resilience-oriented objectives under load, DER, and outage perturbations. Parameter self-adaptation (CARS-MODE) and scenario-set adaptation (SHIELD-MOEA) are orthogonal mechanisms, and the two papers share no experimental claims.

The gap, stated against all three threads, is this: resilience planning studies expose either a single robust worst case or a Pareto front graded on the very scenarios that drove the search; scenario reduction methods select scenarios offline by distributional distance, blind to the evolving population; and hybrid MOEAs adapt operators, not scenario exposure. We are not aware of any prior distribution-planning method that combines (i) search-phase adaptive screening of worst-performing scenarios inside the evolutionary loop, (ii) final evaluation on a scenario set with disjoint random seeds so that screening cannot leak into scoring, and (iii) a worst-case hypervolume readout alongside mean front quality. SHIELD-MOEA is built, and is evaluated, exactly at that intersection.

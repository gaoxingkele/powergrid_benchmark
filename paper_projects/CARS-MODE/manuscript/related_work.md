Three bodies of literature feed directly into CARS-MODE: multi-objective optimization for distribution network expansion and DER/storage siting and sizing, the self-adaptive differential evolution (DE) lineage descending from jDE and SaDE, and constraint-handling techniques — repair operators in particular — for constrained multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs). Reading these threads side by side reveals that each has matured largely in isolation from the other two, which is precisely the seam this paper works in.

## Multi-Objective Optimization for Distribution Expansion and DER/Storage Planning

Planning an active distribution network today means trading capital expenditure against losses, reliability, and renewable hosting capacity, and the field has accordingly shifted from single-objective cost models toward multi-objective and uncertainty-aware formulations. [Prenc, 2024] catalogues the objective functions, constraint sets, and solution principles that recur across active distribution network planning and operation, while [Saldaña-González et al., 2025] survey the modeling elements of recent planning optimization studies and the emerging role of generative methods in scenario construction. Within the mathematical-programming camp, [Liu et al., 2024] handle renewable uncertainty in expansion planning through Wasserstein-distance ambiguity sets with dual relaxation, and [Wang et al., 2025] couple WGAN-GP scenario generation to coordinated source–network–storage expansion. The evolutionary camp is at least as active in the target venues: an improved NSGA-II drives collaborative storage-and-EV planning in [He et al., 2025], an enhanced beluga whale optimizer performs vulnerability-driven storage siting for resilient feeders in [Qi et al., 2025], and a classification-assisted global optimizer jointly places distributed generation, capacitor banks, and charging stations in radial networks in [Alrashidi et al., 2026]. Related formulations extend to reliability-oriented reinforcement with intentional islanding [Alotaibi, 2026], hybrid AC/DC planning for data-center loads under time-series multi-scenario evaluation [Chen et al., 2025], and, on the transmission side, expansion planning via an enhanced coati optimizer [Demirbas et al., 2025]. A pattern emerges from this line of work. Nearly every study wraps a bespoke enhancement around a metaheuristic kernel — most often NSGA-II [Deb et al., 2002], MOEA/D [Zhang and Li, 2007], or a recently named swarm optimizer — with parameters fixed by hand for a single test system, and the underlying case data are frequently proprietary or only partially specified. Community infrastructure for doing better exists: SimBench publishes complete, license-clean benchmark grids designed explicitly for comparable power-flow-based studies [Meinecke et al., 2020], and pandapower provides the open tooling to evaluate them [Thurner et al., 2018], yet planning papers that anchor their entire experimental protocol on such open assets remain rare.

## Self-Adaptive Differential Evolution and Its Power System Uses

DE began as a deceptively simple continuous optimizer [Storn and Price, 1997], but its performance was quickly understood to hinge on the scale factor F, the crossover rate CR, and the mutation strategy — settings that interact with the landscape and cannot be tuned once and for all. The adaptive lineage answers this. jDE encodes F and CR into each individual and lets them evolve by stochastic reset, so good parameter values survive with the solutions they produced [Brest et al., 2006]. SaDE goes a step further and adapts the strategy itself, maintaining a pool of mutation strategies whose selection probabilities are updated from recorded success memories [Qin et al., 2009]. JADE contributes the current-to-pbest mutation and location-adapted parameter sampling with an optional archive [Zhang and Sanderson, 2009], SHADE stabilizes JADE's adaptation through a success-history memory [Tanabe and Fukunaga, 2013], and EPSDE frames the whole question as an ensemble over parameter and strategy pools [Mallipeddi et al., 2011]. The updated survey of [Das et al., 2016] documents how thoroughly these mechanisms outperform fixed configurations on continuous benchmarks. Two transfers of this machinery matter for planning problems, and both are incomplete. The multi-objective transfer started early — DEMO combined DE variation with Pareto-based selection [Robič and Filipič, 2005] — but the adaptive variants above were developed and validated almost exclusively in single-objective settings. The discrete transfer is thinner still: [Pampará et al., 2006] examined how DE's difference-vector arithmetic can be carried into binary spaces and exposed the pitfalls of doing so naively, and the question of what "strategy adaptation" should even mean for binary portfolio variables has received little attention since. In power systems, DE appears mostly in continuous operational problems — a recent example benchmarks DE against competing techniques for economic dispatch with hybrid generation under energy-limitation scenarios [Cadena-Albuja et al., 2025] — whereas the distribution planning studies of the previous thread, whose decision vectors are naturally binary build/no-build portfolios, almost never draw on the jDE/SaDE adaptation toolbox at all.

## Constraint Handling and Repair in Evolutionary Multi-Objective Optimization

How an evolutionary algorithm treats infeasible candidates shapes its search as much as any variation operator. The classical taxonomy — penalties, repair, decoders, and specialized operators — goes back to [Michalewicz and Schoenauer, 1996], and the early single-objective milestones are the parameterless feasibility rules of [Deb, 2000], which need no penalty coefficients, and stochastic ranking, which balances objective and violation information probabilistically [Runarsson and Yao, 2000]; [Coello Coello, 2002] surveys this first generation comprehensively. Within DE specifically, the ε-constrained method with gradient-based mutation showed that relaxing then tightening the feasibility boundary can steer the population through infeasible corridors productively [Takahama and Sakai, 2006]. Repair — actively transforming an infeasible candidate into a feasible one — occupies a curious position in this landscape. The survey of [Salcedo-Sanz, 2009] finds repair highly effective exactly on knapsack-like combinatorial constraints, where a violated budget can be restored by greedy item removal, but also notes that repair operators are problem-specific and can bias the search if applied uniformly. In the constrained multi-objective setting, the field has instead concentrated on selection-side mechanisms: constraint-domination extensions of reference-point sorting [Jain and Deb, 2014], two-phase schemes such as push-and-pull search that ignore constraints before honoring them [Fan et al., 2019], and the many algorithm families organized by the survey of [Liang et al., 2023]. What is largely absent, on both the single- and multi-objective sides, is any coupling between constraint handling and parameter adaptation: repair intensity, when repair is used at all, is a static design choice, never a quantity adapted online from the population's violation state the way F and CR are adapted from its success state.

## Relation to the Companion Project and Shared Generators

The companion project `mintou_p4_shield_resilience_planning` tackles resilience-oriented distribution planning and locates its contribution in the evaluation layer: it screens and prioritizes the stress scenarios against which candidate plans are scored. CARS-MODE holds the scenario set fixed and studies the search loop instead. The projects share generators used to derive SimBench-based benchmark inputs; this common infrastructure is not an independent dataset or result replication. The scientific question in this paper remains independently CARS-MODE-specific: under a fixed proxy benchmark and comparison budget, how does the complete constrained-search framework compare with implemented controls, and which mechanism groups survive ablation?

## Gap Statement

Setting the three threads against each other makes the opening for this work concrete. Distribution planning studies deploy multi-objective metaheuristics with hand-fixed configurations on single, often irreproducible test systems, despite open benchmarks being available. The self-adaptive DE lineage has demonstrated, on continuous single-objective benchmarks, that parameter and strategy adaptation reliably beats fixed settings — but it has not been carried into the binary, multi-objective, budget-constrained territory that planning portfolios occupy. And the constraint-handling literature knows that repair suits budget-type combinatorial constraints better than penalties, yet treats repair as a static bolt-on rather than an adaptable operator. To our knowledge, no prior work combines a jDE-style parameter self-adaptation and SaDE-style success-driven strategy pool with a constraint-aware budget-repair operator inside a binary multi-objective DE, and evaluates the whole on a SimBench-derived, fully public and script-reproducible DER/storage portfolio-proxy benchmark with multi-seed nonparametric significance testing. CARS-MODE is built to occupy exactly that intersection.

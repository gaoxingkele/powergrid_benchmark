Abstract: The integration of renewable energy into Intelligent Distribution Networks (IDNs) is chal-
lenged by the inherent variability and fluctuations in energy supply, particularly with photovoltaic
(PV) generation as the primary form of distributed generation (DG). However, managing the fluctua-
tions and variability in renewable energy supply presents significant challenges. To address these
complexities, it is vital to optimally coordinate flexible resources from source–network–storage–load
(SNSL) in a manner that aligns with cross-sectoral emission reduction strategies while enhancing grid
stability and efficiency. This paper addresses these challenges by proposing a strategy that optimizes
the coordination of PV-based DG, storage, and load resources through Robotic Process Automation
(RPA) to enhance grid stability and support emissions reduction. We use a two-layer dispatching
framework: the lower-layer model, formulated as a quadratic programming problem, maximizes PV
utilization for individual users, while the upper-layer model, based on a second-order cone relaxation
approach, manages the overall IDN to minimize operational costs. The iterative solution leverages
tie-line power flow as boundary information to ensure convergence across the network. Validated
on an enhanced IEEE 33-bus system, the approach demonstrates a 62% increase in PV-based DG
consumption and a 25% reduction in active power losses, highlighting its potential to improve grid
efficiency and contribute to emission reduction goals.
Keywords: emission; robotic process automation; distributed dispatching; resource management
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of smart grid technologies and the increasing integration of
distributed generation (DG) into Intelligent Distribution Networks (IDNs) have introduced
both opportunities and challenges in modern energy systems. Distributed energy sources,
including solar, wind, and other renewable technologies, promise a more sustainable
and decentralized energy future [1,2]. However, managing the inherent fluctuation and
variability in energy supply poses significant challenges to grid stability, reliability, and
economic efficiency [3,4]. The need to optimally coordinate flexible resources, such as
source–network–storage–load (SNSL), in this complex landscape has become critical to
ensure the seamless operation of smart grids [5]. In response, innovative technologies such
as Robotic Process Automation (RPA) have gained prominence for their ability to automate
repetitive tasks, streamline operations, and enhance the adaptability of grid management
systems [6,7].
Previous research has extensively explored methods for optimizing distributed energy
resources within Intelligent Distribution Networks. In [2], economic dispatching methods
were examined with a focus on handling renewable energy uncertainties and demand-
side responses, emphasizing the necessity for adaptive models to manage unpredictable

technologies, a notable gap exists in the integration of RPA into the distributed dispatching

as a strategic tool for managing the complexities of SNSL resources remains underexplored.
Existing models often lack the ability to fully automate resource coordination across mul-
tiple layers of a distribution network, resulting in suboptimal dispatching and delayed
response to real-time fluctuations in energy supply. This study addresses key gaps in
smart grid optimization by integrating Robotic Process Automation (RPA) into distributed

while minimizing operational costs for the IDN, thus enhancing flexibility and efficiency
across network resources. Validated on the IEEE 33-bus system, this approach demon-
strates significant improvements, including a 62% increase in DG consumption and a 25%
reduction in active power losses, highlighting the potential of RPA-driven solutions in
future smart grid management.

2. Related Work
2.1. Robotic Process Automation (RPA)
The use of Robotic Process Automation (RPA) in energy systems is an emerging field.
RPA is recognized for its ability to automate repetitive tasks and streamline operations
in various industries. Ref. [3] proposed a distributionally robust reactive power dispatch
model, incorporating a Wasserstein distance to handle uncertainties in active distribu-
tion networks. Both studies contribute to the development of multi-layered optimization
frameworks that align with the goals of this paper, particularly the integration of RPA into
distributed dispatching. Ref. [7] demonstrated the development of intelligent RPA in a
Brazilian utility case study, where automation enhanced the scalability and coordination
of energy dispatching. Refs. [28,29] explored the impact of RPA on enterprise resource
planning (ERP) systems and risk management in automation, providing insights into how
automation can be applied in complex systems such as smart grids. Ref. [30] provided
valuable insights into the security challenges surrounding Robotic Process Automation
(RPA), which is critical for ensuring the reliability and safety of automated processes in
smart grids. Although the focus is primarily on security, these insights are useful for
strengthening the security framework in RPA-based energy dispatching system, ensuring
a robust and secure operation. Ref. [31], a study on network reconfiguration and voltage
regulation, offered relevant insights for managing unbalanced distribution systems, which
could complement RPA-based dispatching in IDNs. The combination of reconfiguration
and distributed energy resource control aligns well with goal of improving grid flexibility
and stability. Ref. [32] introduced self-learning capabilities for RPA, which could signifi-
cantly enhance the adaptability of the proposed RPA-based dispatching system. Although
their primary application is in business processes, the self-learning mechanisms could be
leveraged to improve real-time decision-making and automation in smart grids. Ref. [33]
provided technical insights into the design and implementation of RPA systems, which
are crucial for understanding how to optimize a RPA-based smart grid dispatching sys-
tem. The system design strategies outlined here could help to improve the scalability and
efficiency of RPA in the model. In [34], a study on the benefits of RPA in supply chains,
the authors highlighted parallels with work in energy systems, emphasizing RPA’s role in
improving process efficiency. The benefit realization techniques discussed could inform
how researchers measure and evaluate the impact of RPA on energy dispatching in a
smart grid model. Ref. [35] covered RPA tools and technologies across various industries,
providing a broad view of their applications, including in energy management. The ex-
ploration of RPA’s industrial applications offers useful insights into how these tools can
be adapted and scaled for energy grid management and optimization. Ref. [36] discussed
the combination of RPA and AI-powered services to increase productivity and reduce
waste, which could inspire the future integration of AI into RPA systems for enhancing op-
timization. Although primarily focused on industrial services, the AI-driven productivity
improvements could be applied to smart grid management for greater efficiency. Ref. [37]
explored RPA’s transformative power in Industry 4.0, offering insights into its potential
to revolutionize energy grid management by automating complex tasks. The focus on
business transformation through RPA could provide a framework for understanding how
RPA can reshape distributed energy management and improve automation in IDNs.

The dispatching model for users with DG focuses on optimizing the generation and
consumption of energy from DG and distributed ES resources to achieve maximum eco-
nomic benefits while maintaining grid stability. The optimization is conducted over a given
time horizon, often divided into time slots (e.g., hourly intervals), based on a time-of-use
(TPU) pricing mechanism that reflects the variation in electricity prices throughout the day.
The primary goal of the user dispatching model is to maximize economic benefits,
which are achieved by optimizing the energy generated by DG, the power stored in or
discharged from the ES system, and the energy exchanged with the grid. The optimization
is impacted by TPU pricing, which determines when it is most cost-effective to sell or
buy electricity.

3. Proposed Model
The optimization of both user and Intelligent Distribution Network (IDN) objectives
within the RPA process is structured in a two-layer model. The lower-layer model aims
to maximize the user’s economic benefit by optimizing the use of distributed generation
(DG) and energy storage (ES) systems. This is achieved by maximizing revenue from
selling electricity, minimizing costs from buying power, and accounting for operational
expenses of photovoltaic (PV) and ES systems over a given time period T. The objective
function in this layer includes terms for selling and buying electricity prices, λs(t) and λb(t),
and the total operating costs, Ctotal(t), which depend on PV generation and ES charging
efficiency. This lower-layer model is solved using quadratic programming, balancing
power flows and SOC within specified limits. In the upper-layer model, the focus shifts
to minimizing the IDN’s total operational costs, incorporating expenses for grid power
purchases, energy storage operations, and system losses, while maintaining voltage stability
and power quality. The objective function includes terms for power exchange with the
upper-level grid, system losses, and device operating costs, managed under a power flow
balance constraint. The optimization process, involving SOPs, SVGs, CBs, and ES systems,
uses an iterative approach where decisions made in the user layer feed into the IDN’s cost
minimization, ensuring that power supply meets demand. The IDN’s model is structured as
a least squares optimization, aiming to balance deviations from optimal power flow paths.
This coordinated, iterative optimization, facilitated by RPA, effectively aligns the economic
objectives of users with the stability and operational efficiency of the IDN, achieving robust
energy management across the smart grid.
The RPA process for the user dispatching model is designed to maximize the user’s
net economic benefit by considering revenue from selling electricity, costs from buying
electricity, and operational costs associated with the PV and ES systems.
For both the user and the distribution network, RPA automates the maximization or
minimization of objective functions (economic benefits for the user and operational costs
for the IDN).

The solving process for both the user and IDN dispatching models involves an it-
erative optimization technique that seeks to find a solution that satisfies the objectives
and constraints of both models, as shown in Figure 2. Since the two systems are inter-

user and the IDN. In this model, Equations (9) and (14) in the lower-layer (user) model
and Equations (10) and (15) in the upper-layer (IDN) model are interconnected as they
collectively work toward optimizing energy usage and minimizing costs within the Intelli-
gent Distribution Network (IDN). Each pair serves a distinct but complementary purpose
in terms of achieving economic efficiency and reducing carbon emissions, with the it-
erative optimization ensuring alignment between individual user benefits and overall
network stability. In essence, Equation (9) defines the target economic objectives for the
user, while Equation (14) provides the means to mathematically optimize the decisions
needed to reach those objectives within the practical constraints of the energy system. In
addition, Equation (10) defines the network’s economic and operational objectives, while
Equation (15) provides the mathematical optimization framework required to achieve these
objectives by balancing supply and demand across the network.

This detailed configuration of the IEEE 32-bus system, with its combination of SOPs,

by 35%, representing a 35% increase in overall system performance. This improvement is
crucial for ensuring that renewable energy is used as efficiently as possible, reducing the
need for additional energy imports from the grid and minimizing carbon emissions.
5. Conclusions
This paper proposes an optimal dispatching method for Intelligent Distribution Net-
works (IDNs) that integrates source–network–storage–load (SNSL) flexible resources, specif-
ically designed to enhance renewable energy consumption and support carbon emission
reduction efforts. The framework is structured into two layers: the lower layer optimizes
dispatch for distributed generation (DG) users, while the upper layer manages the overall
IDN, using tie-line power as boundary information to improve coordination. This approach
not only maximizes renewable DG output but also enhances grid stability and efficiency,
directly contributing to a transition towards a low-carbon energy future.
The key findings from this research include the following:
•
The effective use of tie-line power as boundary information to optimize active and
reactive power coordination across the IDN, enhancing the integration of renewable
energy sources;
•
The integration of flexible resources, particularly Soft Open Points (SOPs), significantly
increased DG consumption and reduced power losses, demonstrating its effectiveness
in redistributing power flows and reducing emissions.
Future research directions that align with this Special Issue include exploring the
dynamic and real-time integration of renewable energy fluctuations and load demand
variations through adaptive rolling optimization algorithms. Additionally, incorporat-
ing advanced forecasting models, such as machine learning algorithms, can further en-
hance the accuracy of DG output and load demand predictions, leading to more efficient
dispatching decisions.

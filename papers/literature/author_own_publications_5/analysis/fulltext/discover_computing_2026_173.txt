1  Introduction
The power system serves as a fundamental pillar of modern society, with its continu­
ous operation being critical for national economic stability and societal well-being. The 
expansion of the grid and the enhancement of operational flexibility have increased the 
complexity of fault detection, posing significant challenges to the reliability and safety 
of power infrastructure. Urban power grids, which are essential for economic activities, 
can incur substantial economic losses and disruptions in the event of faults. Traditional 
fault detection methods, which rely on manual intervention, often struggle to accurately

Abstract
Timely detection and resolution of power equipment failures are vital for ensuring 
the stability and reliability of electrical grids. This paper presents a knowledge graph 
completion model (KGCM) that integrates multi-task learning with pre-trained 
models to enhance fault-handling knowledge graph representations. Entities and 
relations are encoded with a pre-trained model and refined through Translating 
Embeddings (TransE)-based structural constraints, while a Siamese network is 
introduced to improve feature extraction and robustness by predicting missing triplet 
components. The proposed framework was validated on a dataset from the State 
Grid Tianjin as well as standard benchmarks. Experimental results show that KGCM 
consistently outperforms the baseline Language Model Knowledge Embedding with 
TransE (LMKE-TransE) model in terms of Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) and Hits at N 
(Hits@N) metrics. In practical fault-action prediction, KGCM demonstrates superior 
accuracy, enabling more reliable diagnosis and handling of power grid faults. These 
findings confirm the effectiveness of combining pre-trained models, structural 
fine-tuning, and multi-task feature extraction for knowledge graph completion, and 
highlight the model’s potential to enhance automation and dependability in power 
grid fault management.

Contemporary power fault detection algorithms are generally categorized into two 
types: those based on conventional methodologies and those employing deep learning 
techniques [5, 6]. Conventional approaches, dependent on manual detection and inter­
vention, may not effectively discern equipment states or provide timely responses during 
abrupt faults [7, 8]. The integration of knowledge graphs and artificial intelligence offers 
a promising avenue for leveraging deep learning to enhance fault detection and predic­
tion while utilizing knowledge graphs for guidance [9, 10]. However, the effectiveness of 
existing models that combine reinforcement learning with knowledge graph-based fault 
handling is contingent upon the completeness and accuracy of the knowledge graph. 
Inaccuracies or incompleteness in the graph, such as erroneous or missing edges, neces­
sitate refinement. Therefore, the development of an intelligently curated knowledge 
graph is crucial for proficient fault handling [11, 12].
Knowledge graphs are widely used in deep learning frameworks, particularly those 
that emphasize interpretability and causal path elucidation. Nonetheless, current knowl­
edge graph completion algorithms primarily focus on improving representations with­
out fully leveraging latent edges or correcting redundant ones, which may impact the 
accuracy of fault detection and handling. Additionally, while numerous studies incorpo­
rate pre-trained large language models for knowledge graph representation [13], these 
models often lack domain-specific structured knowledge constraints and may exhibit 
robustness issues in feature extraction. Hence, a framework that strengthens domain-
specific knowledge graph representation and enhances feature extraction robustness is 
essential for improving the accuracy of fault handling automation.
This paper proposes a Multi-Task Knowledge Graph Completion Framework (KGCM) 
designed to improve the effective representation of fault-handling knowledge graphs and 
enhance the robustness of feature extraction networks. KGCM refines and augments 
pre-trained model knowledge graph representations based on domain-specific knowl­
edge constraints using TransE and employs Siamese neural networks to enhance feature 
extraction robustness in predicting unknown triplet information. By doing so, KGCM 
enriches the representation of fault-handling knowledge graphs, thereby improving the 
reliability of downstream automated fault detection and handling.
The contributions of this study are as follows:
Enhancement of fault-handling knowledge graph representation through the integra­
tion of domain-specific knowledge constraints and pre-trained models.
Improvement of feature extraction capabilities in predicting triplet processes within 
fault-handling knowledge graphs using Siamese neural networks.
Application of the multi-task learning-based knowledge graph completion framework 
to fault-handling knowledge graph representation and completion tasks, providing a 
robust foundation for downstream applications.

The use of multi-task learning enhances the accuracy and robustness of embedding 
models, aiding in the correction of node and edge representations within knowledge 
graphs. This framework not only offers a superior foundation for subsequent tasks but 
also has valuable applications in various power grid operation and maintenance sce­

approaches that merely stack language models with knowledge embeddings (such as 
standard BERT + TransE or LMKE), the proposed KGCM framework is specifically engi­

priors, they often lack discriminative power when facing sparse and highly similar fault 
samples in the grid. By leveraging a multi-task learning mechanism, KGCM enforces a 
rigorous similarity verification via the Siamese network alongside semantic completion. 
This design effectively distinguishes confusing fault patterns, thereby solving the issue of 
insufficient robustness in traditional models on long-tail data and ensuring the high reli­
ability required for safety–critical power systems.

2  Related works
2.1  Knowledge graph completion
Knowledge graph completion is a critical task in knowledge representation and rea­
soning, garnering significant attention in recent years. Various methodologies and 
techniques have been proposed to address this task. Meilicke et al. [14] introduced an 
anytime bottom-up technique that excels in learning logical rules from extensive knowl­
edge graphs, outperforming conventional rule-based approaches. Another notable 
advancement is Knowledge Graph BERT(KG-BERT), proposed by Yao et al. [15], which 
utilizes pre-trained language models to represent triples in knowledge graphs as textual 
sequences. Similarly, Zhang et al. [16] introduced hypercomplex representations for 
knowledge graph embeddings, achieving state-of-the-art performance on established 
benchmarks. Knowledge acquisition, particularly in the context of knowledge graph 
completion, has been a focal point of research. Ji et al. [17] conducted a comprehen­
sive review of techniques, including embedding methods, path inference, and logical 
rule reasoning. Recently, Zhang et al. [18] proposed a Relational Graph Neural Network 
with Hierarchical Attention, aimed at inferring missing values in incomplete knowledge 
triples. Addressing the limitations of existing graph embedding approaches, Wang et 
al. [19] enhanced textual encoding with graph embedding techniques to achieve more 
efficient knowledge graph completion. Additionally, Zhang et al. [20] presented a neu­
ral network-based approach for drug repurposing for COVID-19, leveraging knowledge 
graph completion techniques. Overall, knowledge graph completion is crucial in knowl­
edge representation and reasoning, driving the development of innovative approaches to 
improve performance and accuracy in inferring missing values.

2.2  Power grid fault diagnosis based on knowledge graph
The integration of knowledge graph technology into power grid equipment fault diag­
nosis has gained considerable attention in recent years. Yun et al. [21] focused on devel­
oping a knowledge question-and-answer system for diagnosing faults in the Electric 
Information Collection System, leveraging knowledge graph technology to enhance rea­
soning efficiency and support the intellectualization of fault diagnosis. Huang et al. [22] 
emphasized the construction of knowledge graphs for large-scale power grids in China 
to improve operational, maintenance, and management efficiency. Bo et al. [23] dem­
onstrated the effectiveness of a method based on understanding fault disposal preplan 
knowledge, reducing fault disposal pressure and improving efficiency. Knowledge graph 
technology is also applied to fault diagnosis within specific components of the power 
grid system. For example, Chan et al. [24] explored power oscillation detection and clas­
sification using telemetry data monitoring systems based on graph theory. Li et al. [25] 
introduced a dynamic graph-based anomaly detection algorithm for the electrical grid, 
enabling the rapid identification of unusual events through sensor data analysis. Zhou et 
al. [26] developed a fault model for power communication equipment and constructed 
an equipment fault knowledge graph for visual representation. Moreover, Wu et al. [27] 
proposed a knowledge graph technology-based fault diagnosis framework for HVDC 
transmission systems to address limitations in existing fault diagnosis methods. Liu et al. 
[28] detailed the application of knowledge graphs in smart grid fault diagnosis, empha­
sizing knowledge extraction, fusion, processing, and the construction of fault schedul­
ing knowledge graphs for effective fault diagnosis systems. In summary, the literature 
review highlights the growing interest and application of knowledge graph technology in 
enhancing fault diagnosis processes for power grid equipment.
Furthermore, recent research increasingly focuses on integrating explicit domain 
knowledge and domain adaptation techniques to address data scarcity and distribution 
shifts in fault diagnosis. For instance, Ren et al. proposed a Physics-guided TL-LSTM 
network for lithium-ion battery degradation prediction, effectively combining physical 
laws with transfer learning to enhance generalization capabilities [41]. Similarly, a com­
prehensive survey on domain adaptation for intelligent fault diagnosis emphasized the 
critical role of transferring diagnostic knowledge across varying working conditions and 
equipment types [42, 43]. Drawing inspiration from these domain-adaptive paradigms, 
our KGCM framework employs the knowledge graph itself as a structured domain con­
straint. Unlike traditional statistical adaptation, we utilize multi-task learning to align 
the semantic representations of long-tail fault samples with the verified topological 
structure of the grid, effectively solving the domain adaptation challenge in reliable fault 
handling.

aging discriminative gradient information. In the field of natural language process­
ing, Tang et al. [30] demonstrated the effectiveness of Bidirectional Long Short-Term 
Memory Networks (BiLSTM) in improving text semantic understanding. In the context

3  Proposed model
3.1  Framework
This section presents a framework for knowledge graph completion in power grid fault 
handling based on multi-task learning, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
The proposed KGCM framework is not a simple concatenation of pre-trained models 
and structured embedding modules; rather, it is a multi-task synergistic system designed 
to tackle the challenge of long-tail distributions in power equipment faults. As illustrated 
in Fig. 1, this framework advances beyond existing methods like LMKE [37] by funda­
mentally optimizing their discriminatory capability for low-frequency, high-risk faults. 
Our core design philosophy operates on two levels: First, we employ the joint encoding 
of pre-trained language models (BERT) and TransE to mitigate knowledge graph spar­
sity, providing global semantic and structural constraints. Second, and most critically, 
we introduce a parallel Siamese network module to perform fine-grained verification in 
the feature space. Traditional LMKE models rely on MLP-based contrastive learning, 
which performs well with ample samples but tends to misclassify long-tail fault samples 
due to blurred feature boundaries. KGCM injects the strong discriminatory power of 
the Siamese network into the embedding process via multi-task learning, compelling 
the model to perform a rigorous "semantic-structural-feature" triple verification during

The following sections will detail three key components of the framework. First, the 
foundational LMKE model utilized in this study will be introduced. Second, the use of 
the TransE structured encoding network combined with pre-trained models for knowl­
edge graph representation will be explained. Finally, the integration of Siamese neural 
networks to improve the feature extraction module will be discussed. In this framework, 
the large language model processes the initial representations of entities and relations 
within the triplets, incorporating both pre-trained knowledge and descriptive informa­
tion. This approach generates encoded vectors h, r, and t in Rd, integrating entity and 
relation descriptions with other context-related information from BERT, thus enhancing 
the overall representation beyond intrinsic data.
LMKE outperforms previous models by not only improving the effective representa­
tion of triplets through the use of large language models and additional triplet descrip­
tions but also by refining negative sampling strategies. Unlike earlier approaches, LMKE 
treats the triplet itself as the positive sample within the current batch, while the known 
data of the triplet and other targets from the batch, excluding the positive target, are 
used as negative samples. This novel methodology optimizes the negative sampling and 
encoding processes, enhancing the model framework's efficiency. For link prediction, 
LMKE replaces traditional cosine similarity-based calculations with a multi-layer per­
ceptron for measuring compatibility. Additionally, to enhance the distinction between 
entity representations, the model includes features such as node degrees during the 
encoding of known data features. These refinements improve the scientific rigor and 
accuracy of triplet representations within the knowledge graph.

After the pre-trained model generates entity features and relationships for the triplets, 
TransE is utilized to fine-tune these representations, enhancing their domain-specific 
and structural semantics.
The objective of this module is to enhance the encoding of triplets derived from pre-
trained large-scale models by leveraging the structural insights provided by TransE and 
the domain-specific knowledge embedded in the data. This approach addresses limi­
tations inherent in existing frameworks for encoding knowledge graph triplets, which 
often rely heavily on pre-trained models that prioritize generic semantic information. 
Such models may overlook domain-specific, relational, and structural details, potentially 
leading to suboptimal performance in knowledge graph applications. Consequently, this 
study integrates the TransE model to fine-tune these existing models, thereby improving 
their effectiveness in encoding knowledge graph triplets.
To begin, each triplet is meticulously defined along with its descriptive information, 
constructing a sequence format suitable for input into pre-trained large-scale models. A 
critical aspect of this process is the integration of descriptive details with the triplet ele­
ments to form a coherent sequence structure for model input. These sequences are then 
processed by the pre-trained model. During this process, the BERT model generates rep­
resentations for entities and relations within each triplet, as well as an overall sequence 
representation denoted by the Classification (CLS) token. The CLS token output primar­
ily serves semantic representation and prediction tasks for the entire sequence, while the 
triplet representations can be utilized for link prediction or entity prediction tasks.
Given the specific requirements of knowledge graph triplet encoding tasks, particular 
emphasis is placed on refining the representations of triplets h, r, and t. These outputs 
are used as inputs for the TransE model, which performs structural semantic encoding 
to refine the overall model. The TransE model can be employed to integrate the struc­
tural relationships between triplets, thereby enhancing their representational efficacy 
within the knowledge graph. The TransE scoring function is defined as follows:

We address this by utilizing the TransE scoring function L = ∑||h + r − t|| as a structural

model to be projected into a geometric space that strictly respects the grid's topology. 
For instance, in the power domain, a "Transformer" entity must logically utilize a "con­

action), the TransE component generates a high energy loss. This penalty effectively 
"pulls" the vector representations back into a valid structural configuration during back-
propagation, thereby ensuring that the final embeddings satisfy the logical constraints 
inherent to power grid operations.
By leveraging the characteristics of the TransE model, triplets are treated as mech­
anisms for capturing relational transmissions between entities, effectively capturing 
semantic correlations and structural nuances. Consequently, incorporating fine-tuning 
with the TransE model on top of pre-trained large-scale models allows for a more com­
prehensive exploitation of structural insights and domain-specific knowledge within the 
knowledge graph, resulting in more precise and nuanced representations of knowledge 
graph triplets.

TransE fine-tuning stage by means of relation weighting and rule-based sample control.

for static topological relations), so that optimization focuses on fault–status–action 
patterns rather than background structure. In addition, basic operational and temporal 
rules derived from grid regulations are used to filter positives and construct hard nega­

rule-violating negatives, while corrupted triples that still satisfy the rules are discarded

3.3  Feature extraction based on siamese network
In the context of power grid fault management, knowledge graph completion is not only 
a generic link prediction problem but also a similarity-driven decision problem: opera­
tors need to infer appropriate handling actions for a current fault by referring to histori­
cally similar fault–equipment–action patterns. However, many real-world fault records 
share very similar textual descriptions (e.g., alarms, protection actions, device states) 
while corresponding to different root causes or recommended actions due to differences 
in equipment type, network location, or operating conditions. If only a single encoder 
and a simple scoring function are used, semantically similar but operationally different 
triplets may be mapped too closely in the embedding space, which can lead to erroneous 
action recommendations.

Page 9 of 19
Huang et al. Discover Computing          (2026) 29:173 
Siamese networks are well suited to this setting because they learn a discriminative 
similarity metric between pairs of inputs under explicit supervision. By feeding pairs of 
triplets (or query–candidate pairs) into two weight-sharing branches and enforcing dis­
tance constraints according to whether they represent consistent or inconsistent fault-
handling patterns, the Siamese architecture pulls together triplets that correspond to 
genuinely similar fault scenarios and actions, and pushes apart triplets that are super­
ficially similar in text but operationally different in the grid. This directly matches the 
requirements of power fault knowledge graph completion, where fine-grained discrimi­
nation between similar fault cases is crucial for safe and reliable decision support.
Following the establishment of the foundational framework for triplet representa­
tion within the knowledge graph, an opportunity was identified to optimize negative 
sampling and the matching process of known triplet data with unknown target entities 
within the LMKE contrastive learning framework. To address this, a Siamese network 
module was introduced, capitalizing on its advantages in matching algorithms and fea­
ture extraction.
The explicit integration of the Siamese network is primarily motivated by the long-tail 
distribution inherent in power grid fault data. In practical scenarios, normal operation 
samples are abundant, while specific fault instances are sparse, presenting a classic "few-
shot" learning challenge. Standard classifiers often struggle to generalize in such imbal­
anced contexts. In contrast, Siamese networks are fundamentally suited for One-shot 
or Few-shot learning via metric learning [44]. By focusing on minimizing the distance 
between positive pairs and maximizing it for negative ones, the model learns a robust 
similarity function rather than a rigid class boundary. This capability allows KGCM to 
capture subtle feature discrepancies in rare fault events, significantly enhancing robust­
ness. Furthermore, the utilization of identical MLP structures with shared weights in 
both branches is a standard and critical design in Siamese architectures [45]. This weight 
sharing ensures that both the query and target representations are projected into the 
unified latent feature space, making the subsequent distance metric calculation geomet­
rically valid and semantically comparable. The framework is illustrated in Fig. 3.

thereby enhancing the robustness of feature extraction and improving the accuracy of 
triplet representation and completion within the knowledge graph.

3.4  Training strategy
To fully exploit the complementary strengths of semantic encoding, structural con­
straints, and discriminative feature extraction, we adopt a Joint Training (End-to-End)

strategy rather than a multi-stage pipeline. The entire KGCM framework is optimized 
simultaneously to ensure that the pre-trained embeddings are dynamically adjusted 
to satisfy both the structural rules of the power grid and the feature discriminability 
requirements. The global objective function is defined as a weighted sum of the losses 
from the three components:
LTotal = LLMKE + λLTransE + γLsiamese
(8)
where LLMKE represents the contrastive loss from the language model-based embed­
ding task, LTransE denotes the structural scoring loss defined in Eq. (1), and Lsiamese cor­
responds to the distance-based loss from the Siamese network. The hyperparameters λ 
and γ control the relative importance of the structural constraints and the feature verifi­
cation task, respectively. During backpropagation, gradients from all three loss compo­
nents flow back to update the shared parameters of the pre-trained encoder, ensuring a 
unified and robust representation space.
4  Experimental settings and analysis
4.1  Dataset and data processing
This study utilized a dataset obtained from the State Grid Tianjin, formatted in CSV, 
which includes status information on power grid equipment, timestamps, associated 
records, and corresponding labels for each equipment state. Initial data cleaning was 
performed to reduce noise during model training by filtering out nodes with excessive or 
insufficient information. After data cleaning, we construct the fault-handling knowledge 
graph from the raw logs in a rule-based manner. Each time-stamped record is mapped 
to an equipment entity via its SCADA/dispatching code, and to standardized status and 
action labels according to the enterprise fault-handling guideline. We define three main 
entity types (Equipment, Status, Action) and two core relations: status-of (Status, Equip­
ment) and action-on (Action, Equipment). For each valid record, we generate a status 
triplet ⟨equipment, status-of, status⟩ and, when a handling operation is recorded, an 
action triplet ⟨equipment, action-on, action⟩; records with unmapped or ambiguous 
codes are discarded. Although the final graph is static, all records are sorted by time­
stamp per equipment, and only temporally consistent status–action pairs (e.g., fault fol­
lowed by restore within a reasonable window) are retained, while sequences violating 
basic temporal logic are removed.
4.2  Knowledge graph construction
To ensure the reproducibility of our graph structure, we detail the construction pipeline 
from raw SCADA logs to the structured knowledge graph:
Triplet Extraction Rules: We employed a rule-based mapping strategy to convert tabu­
lar CSV records into subject-predicate-object triples. Specifically, discrete attributes in 
the logs were mapped to entity relations. For instance, a log entry Row(ID: 101, Status: 
Alarm) is transformed into the triplet (Device_101, has_status, Alarm). Similarly, the 
relationship between equipment and its corresponding handling action is modeled as 
(Device_101, takes_action, Manual-inspection).
Entity Alignment: Unique Equipment IDs were extracted and mapped to distinct 
graph nodes. We utilized a unified dictionary to align identifiers from different subsys­

Page 12 of 19
Huang et al. Discover Computing          (2026) 29:173 
tems, ensuring that Device_101 in the alarm log refers to the same node as Device_101 
in the topology records.
Temporal Processing: Although the original dataset contains timestamps, the objec­
tive of this study is to capture the intrinsic logical correlations between fault states and 
actions. Therefore, we constructed a static snapshot of the power grid by aggregating 
equipment states over the observation window. Temporal information was primar­
ily used to filter out transient noise and ensure state consistency during aggregation, 
rather than embedding time as a dynamic attribute in the graph.

4.4  Parameter setting
Our methods were compared with LMKE [37], which aims to improve the representa­
tion of less common entities while addressing the limitations of previous description-
centered methods. LMKE introduces a knowledge embedding (KE) learning framework 
that integrates descriptions within a contrastive learning paradigm, thereby enhancing 
training and evaluation efficiency.
In this experimental setup, all models were implemented using PyTorch and Python 
3.8. The AdamW optimizer was adopted, and the pre-trained Bidirectional Encoder Rep­
resentations from Transformers (BERT) model bert-base-uncased was used as the text 
encoder. The learning rate for the pre-trained BERT was set to 1e−5, whereas the learn­
ing rate for the remaining trainable parameters was set to 5e−4. Batch normalization was 
applied to accelerate convergence, with a batch size of 16.
To select the number of training epochs, we first monitored the validation MRR on 
each dataset. On UMLS, the validation performance continued to improve beyond 20

epochs and typically stabilized between 80 and 100 epochs, so we set the maximum 
number of epochs to 100. On WN18RR and the power grid dataset, the models con­
verged much faster and reached stable validation performance within about 15–20 
epochs; for these datasets we therefore trained for 21 epochs, which is slightly beyond 
the observed convergence point. All experiments were run on the same hardware plat­
form to ensure fair comparison across methods.

models for knowledge graph completion, has demonstrated notable effectiveness across 
different contexts. This study introduces the KGCM algorithm, which builds upon 
LMKE by incorporating incremental refinements and validates its performance using 
three datasets: WN18RR, UMLS, and a power grid fault handling dataset. Since the 
experiments were conducted as single runs, the results reported in Table 3 are without

handling domain-specific fault patterns.
As indicated by the data in Table 3, the KGCM model provides slight improvements

4.6  Ablation study on the Siamese matcher
To isolate the effect of the Siamese matcher from the multi-task setting, we compare 
three variants in Table  4, namely LMKE-TransE (pre-trained encoder + TransE fine-
tuning with the original single-branch MLP matcher), KGCM w/o Siamese (multi-task 
setting retained but still using the original MLP matcher), and KGCM (full) (multi-
task + TransE + Siamese matcher as our final model). On WN18RR, KGCM w/o Siamese 
improves MRR from 0.4508 to 0.4591 over LMKE-TransE, and the full KGCM with the 
Siamese matcher further increases MRR to 0.4667, with a similar trend on Hits@10. On 
the power grid dataset, we observe consistent but moderate gains (from 0.2665 to 0.2732 
and 0.2801 in MRR). These results demonstrate that the multi-task setting provides a 
solid improvement over the baseline, and the proposed Siamese matcher brings an addi­
tional, stable benefit without relying on overly aggressive architectural changes.
The results in Table 4 indicate that removing the Siamese module leads to a perfor­
mance drop (MRR decreases by approximately 0.6%), validating its role in enhancing fea­
ture discrimination for long-tail samples. Furthermore, the exclusion of TransE results 
in a more significant decline, underscoring the necessity of domain-specific structural 
constraints for accurate embedding.

employed. Regarding the training duration, we utilized an Early Stopping strategy based 
on the validation set MRR to determine the optimal number of epochs and prevent 
overfitting.
 	•
UMLS: Due to the dataset's smaller scale and sparsity, the model required more 
iterations to converge, resulting in 100 epochs.
 	•
WN18RR & Power Grid: These datasets exhibited faster convergence patterns; 
thus, training was stopped at 21 epochs to maintain optimal generalization. The 
checkpoint with the highest validation performance was selected for testing.
5  Experimental details
To ensure reproducibility, we document additional implementation details. The Siamese 
network adopted a 3-layer MLP architecture in each branch to enhance feature extrac­
tion robustness. The TransE module was configured with a margin value of 1.0. In our 
implementation, TransE employs the L2 norm squared as the scoring function, which 
provided more stable convergence compared to the L1 norm. Furthermore, early stop­
ping was applied with a patience of 10 epochs based on the validation MRR metric, and 
the checkpoint with the highest validation MRR was selected for reporting.
6  Conclusion
This study presents the KGCM framework, an advanced deep learning algorithm 
designed to improve knowledge graph completion specifically for power equipment 
fault management through its multitasking capabilities. Unlike previous approaches that 
either rely solely on deep learning techniques for fault detection and handling or focus 
on completing entities and relationships within knowledge graphs, the KGCM frame­
work excels in knowledge graph representation. The framework meticulously fine-tunes 
pre-trained models by incorporating domain-specific structural constraints, which 
enhances semantic understanding from existing models while preserving domain-spe­
cific context in the final representations. Additionally, the integration of Siamese neural 
networks enhances feature extraction during triple prediction, leading to more accurate 

Page 17 of 19
Huang et al. Discover Computing          (2026) 29:173 
predictions of unknown information within triples and improving the overall effective­
ness and accuracy of the knowledge graph completion. Empirical validation confirms 
the effectiveness of the KGCM model in advancing knowledge graph completion for 
power equipment fault management. By generating a comprehensive knowledge graph 
of power equipment faults, the framework provides more informed guidance for fault 
management actions, yielding practical outcomes. This enhancement translates directly 
into improved reliability and operational robustness for real-world power grid applica­
tions. Recent studies on knowledge-graph-based [38] fault diagnosis frameworks also 
highlight the importance of structured knowledge in improving power system reliability, 
supporting the direction of our proposed method [39, 40].
While the proposed KGCM framework demonstrates strong performance in knowl­
edge graph completion and fault management, its effectiveness can degrade when the 
underlying knowledge graph data is highly incomplete or contains substantial noise. In 
such scenarios, the mode’s ability to accurately infer missing triplet components and 
support fault diagnosis may be compromised. This is because the structural cues and 
relational patterns that KGCM leverages become sparse or unreliable. Future work will 
focus on integrating noise-robust learning strategies and graph denoising techniques 
to address these challenges and enhance model applicability in real-world power grid 
environments.
Future research will explore the field of human–computer interaction to endow the 
algorithmic framework with autonomous capabilities for addressing minor issues and 
developing collaborative strategies for tackling major challenges. Emphasis will also be 
placed on optimizing the algorithm for greater efficiency in both temporal and spatial 
dimensions to enhance computational effectiveness.
